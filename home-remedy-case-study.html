01 Overview

A trusted contractor with
a site that didn't show it

Home Remedy is a Houston home remodeling contractor serving Spring Branch and the greater Houston area. With a 4.9-star Google rating, Houzz awards, and a proven track record in bathroom, kitchen, and exterior remodeling — the quality of their work was never in question. Their website was.

The existing site used bold, saturated colors that made navigation visually overwhelming, failed to communicate their craftsmanship, and offered a basic RFQ form that attracted poorly-qualified leads. The goal: redesign the site to reflect the quality of the business — and make the most-requested service, bathroom remodeling, easier to find and explore.

"They wanted to revamp and modernize their site and improve lead generation specifically targeting qualified bathroom remodeling leads."

— Project Brief, Home Remedy LLC

— Problem Statement

How can UX design help a local remodeling business attract higher-quality leads while better reflecting the quality of their work?

By improving visual credibility, restructuring the service navigation around the highest-demand category (bathroom remodeling), and redesigning the RFQ form to capture better-qualified submissions, UX can directly improve both lead quality and conversion volume.

02 Research

Understanding
homeowner intent

Before touching design, I collaborated with the marketing and analytics teams to understand where leads were coming from and how users were interacting with the site. I then conducted stakeholder Q&A sessions to align on business goals and desired outcomes.

Research Artifacts
01
Analytics Review
Audited 6 months of site traffic and conversion data. Found that 44% of all form submissions were specifically about bathroom remodeling — confirming that the nav structure needed to surface this service prominently, not bury it under a generic "Remodeling Services" dropdown.
02
Stakeholder Q&A
One-on-one sessions with the client revealed their desire for an "elegant" use of their existing teal and orange palette — citing local competitors who used bright, white-centric designs. They also wanted to prominently feature their 4.9-star Google rating and Houzz awards.
03
Competitor Analysis
Reviewed local and regional home remodeling sites to understand how top performers presented service hierarchies, photography, and trust signals. Light, professional photography paired with clear service breakdowns consistently outperformed bold, dark-heavy layouts in this category.
— Key Findings
↗ Finding 01
Bold colors were creating navigation friction
The existing dark, saturated palette made the site feel visually overwhelming and difficult to scan. Users struggled to distinguish between sections, slowing their path to the services they were looking for.
↗ Finding 02
Bathroom remodeling was undersold in navigation
Despite driving 44% of all form leads, bathroom remodeling was not broken out into specific sub-services (bathtub, shower, vanities). Users had to call to understand their options — adding unnecessary friction before contact.
↗ Finding 03
The RFQ form was attracting unqualified leads
A basic, generic inquiry form with minimal fields generated leads that required extensive back-and-forth to qualify. A more specific form structure was needed to surface intent and project scope upfront.
↗ Finding 04
Trust signals were present but buried
A 4.9-star Google rating and multiple Houzz awards were among the business's strongest differentiators — but they were not prominently featured. Homeowners evaluating contractors need social proof above the fold.

Challenges Navigated
  • Retain the client's existing teal and orange brand colors while making them feel refined, not overwhelming
  • Restructure a flat nav into a hierarchy that properly surfaces bathroom sub-services
  • Redesign the RFQ form to capture more qualified leads without creating form fatigue
  • Source and integrate professional photography that matched the elevated brand direction
Design Decisions Made
  • Shifted to lighter tints and shades of teal and amber on white backgrounds for an "elegant" feel
  • Created dedicated sub-pages for Bathtub, Shower Remodeling, and Custom Vanities under Bathroom Remodeling
  • Added service-specific fields and a message prompt to the RFQ form to pre-qualify intent
  • Collaborated with copywriters to produce additional SEO content for each new sub-page

05 Solutions

Six design decisions
built for homeowner trust

Elevated Visual Design Expanded Bathroom Navigation Redesigned RFQ Form Professional Photography Trust Signal Placement Interactive Service Sections
01
Elevated Visual Design — Light, Professional Aesthetic
The original site relied on bold, dark saturation that made navigation feel overwhelming. The redesign shifted to a clean, white-dominant layout using lighter tints of the teal and amber palette — allowing professional photography to carry visual weight. The updated logo and consistent brand application across pages created a polished, cohesive identity that better reflected the quality of the work.
↑ Improved brand-to-quality perception per stakeholder review
02
Expanded Bathroom Navigation — Sub-Service Pages
Bathroom remodeling drove 44% of all form leads, yet the existing site treated it as a single menu item with no breakdown. The redesign introduced dedicated intermediate and detail pages for Bathtub Remodeling, Shower Remodeling, and Custom Vanities — giving users the specific information they needed without having to call. This directly addressed the client's top-stated pain point.
↑ 3 new sub-service pages created for highest-demand service category
03
Redesigned RFQ Form — Qualified Lead Capture
The original form was generic — name, email, message — which produced broad, unqualified submissions. The redesigned RFQ added a "Service of Interest" dropdown, zip code field, and a file attachment option, allowing prospects to share project context upfront. This shift reduced back-and-forth qualification time for the client and improved the ratio of actionable leads to total submissions.
↑ More specific leads per submission vs. prior generic form
04
Trust Signals Above the Fold
The 4.9-star Google rating, Houzz Best of 2018, and Angie's List awards were buried or absent from key entry points. In the redesign, all three trust signals were surfaced in the top navigation bar and repeated in an "Award-Winning Service" section below the hero — creating immediate credibility for first-time visitors who don't yet know the brand.
4.9★ Google rating surfaced in nav + 3 award badges above fold

07 Reflection

What I'd do
differently

Working on this project gave me a clear window into what local homeowners are looking for when evaluating a remodeling partner: trust, reliability, and evidence of quality. They will not wait for you to earn their confidence — if the site doesn't convey it immediately, they move to the next result. That reality shaped every design decision, from surfacing the Google rating above the fold to ensuring professional photography carried visual authority across every page.

If I were starting again, I'd push for usability testing specifically around the RFQ flow — with real homeowners, not just stakeholder assumptions. The form restructure was grounded in analytics and stakeholder insight, but direct user observation would have given us stronger evidence for the specific field choices and ordering. Earlier testing would also have surfaced mobile interaction issues before QA, rather than during it.

The other area I'd revisit: the branding colors. While the refinements made the palette feel significantly more sophisticated, I believe a more comprehensive overhaul — moving to a cooler, crisper teal with a warm neutral accent — would better position Home Remedy at the premium end of the local contractor market. That's a conversation worth revisiting in a future phase.
