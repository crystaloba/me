Home Remedy LLC redesign

Adding sophistication to a Houston home remodeling contractor's website: modernizing an outdated UI, expanding bathroom service categories, and redesigning the RFQ flow to sharpen lead quality and conversion.

Role
UX Designer
Tools
Figma
Client
Home Remedy LLC, Houston, TX
Industry
Home remodeling, local services

Overview

Redesigning a site to reflect the luxury clients they were wanting to attract

Home Remedy is a Houston home remodeling contractor serving Spring Branch and the greater Houston area. They came to us wanting a fresh, modern look for their website and a stronger pipeline of leads.

The existing site used bold, saturated colors that made navigation visually overwhelming, failed to communicate their craftsmanship, and offered a basic RFQ form that attracted poorly-qualified leads.

They wanted to revamp and modernize their site and improve lead generation, specifically targeting qualified bathroom remodeling leads.

Problem statement

How can UX design help a local remodeling business attract higher-quality leads while better reflecting the quality of their work?

Research

Understanding homeowner intent

Before touching design, I collaborated with the marketing and analytics teams to understand where leads were coming from and how users were interacting with the site. I then conducted stakeholder Q&A sessions to align on business goals and desired outcomes.

  • Analytics review

    Audited six months of site traffic and conversion data. Found that 44% of all form submissions were specifically about bathroom remodeling, confirming that the nav structure needed to surface this service prominently, not bury it under a generic "Remodeling Services" dropdown.

  • Stakeholder Q&A

    One-on-one sessions with the client revealed their desire for an "elegant" use of their existing teal and orange palette, citing local competitors who used bright, white-centric designs. They also wanted to prominently feature their 4.9-star Google rating and Houzz awards.

  • Competitor analysis

    Reviewed local and regional home remodeling sites to understand how top performers presented service hierarchies, photography, and trust signals. Light, professional photography paired with clear service breakdowns consistently outperformed bold, dark-heavy layouts in this category.

What we found

Bold colors were creating friction

The existing dark, saturated palette made the site feel visually overwhelming and difficult to scan. Users struggled to distinguish between sections, slowing their path to the services they were looking for.

Bathroom remodeling was undersold

Despite driving 44% of all form leads, bathroom remodeling was not broken out into specific sub-services (bathtub, shower, vanities). Users had to call to understand their options, adding unnecessary friction before contact.

The RFQ form attracted unqualified leads

A basic, generic inquiry form with minimal fields generated leads that required extensive back-and-forth to qualify. A more specific form structure was needed to surface intent and project scope upfront.

Trust signals were buried

A 4.9-star Google rating and multiple Houzz awards were among the business's strongest differentiators, but they weren't prominently featured. Homeowners evaluating contractors need social proof above the fold.

Constraints and the decisions they led to

Constraints

  • Retain the client's existing teal and orange brand colors while making them feel refined, not overwhelming
  • Restructure a flat nav into a hierarchy that properly surfaces bathroom sub-services
  • Redesign the RFQ form to capture more qualified leads without creating form fatigue
  • Source and integrate professional photography that matched the elevated brand direction

Decisions

  • Shifted to lighter tints and shades of teal and amber on white backgrounds for an "elegant" feel
  • Created dedicated sub-pages for bathtub, shower, and custom vanity remodeling
  • Added service-specific fields and a message prompt to the RFQ form to pre-qualify intent
  • Collaborated with copywriters to produce additional SEO content for each new sub-page

Branding

Elegant use of existing brand colors

Rather than a full brand overhaul, the design refined what already existed, shifting the palette toward lighter tints and shades to achieve a premium, airy aesthetic without losing brand recognition. Typography moved to Europa for headings and Arboria for body text, adding clean sophistication.

Solutions

Here are the four solutions we proposed

  • Elevated visual design

    A light, professional aesthetic

    The original site relied on bold, dark saturation that made navigation feel overwhelming. The redesign shifted to a clean, white-dominant layout using lighter tints of the teal and amber palette, allowing professional photography to carry visual weight.

    Improved brand-to-quality perception, per stakeholder review

  • Expanded bathroom navigation

    Sub-service pages

    Bathroom remodeling drove 44% of all form leads, yet the existing site treated it as a single menu item with no breakdown. The redesign introduced dedicated pages for bathtub, shower, and custom vanity remodeling, giving users the specific information they needed without having to call.

    Three new sub-service pages created for the highest-demand category

  • Redesigned RFQ form

    Qualified lead capture

    The original form was generic (name, email, message), which produced broad, unqualified submissions. The redesigned RFQ added a "service of interest" dropdown, zip code field, and a file attachment option, letting prospects share project context upfront.

    More specific leads per submission vs. the prior generic form

  • Trust signals above the fold

    Credibility at first glance

    The 4.9-star Google rating, Houzz Best of 2018, and Angie's List awards were buried or absent from key entry points. The redesign surfaced all three in the top navigation and in an "award-winning service" section below the hero.

    4.9★ Google rating surfaced in nav, plus three award badges above the fold

Reflection

What I'd do differently

Working on this project gave me a clear window into what local homeowners are looking for when evaluating a remodeling partner: trust, reliability, and evidence of quality. They will not wait for you to earn their confidence. If the site doesn't convey it immediately, they move to the next result.

If I were starting again, I'd push for usability testing specifically around the RFQ flow, with real homeowners, not just stakeholder assumptions. The form restructure was grounded in analytics and stakeholder insight, but direct user observation would have given us stronger evidence for the specific field choices and ordering.

The other area I'd revisit: the branding colors. While the refinements made the palette feel significantly more sophisticated, I believe a more comprehensive overhaul (moving to a cooler, crisper teal with a warm neutral accent) would better position Home Remedy at the premium end of the local contractor market.
