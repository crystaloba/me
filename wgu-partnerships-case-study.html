WGU Strategic Partnerships redesign

Redesigning WGU's partner-facing experience to end audience confusion, show ROI evidence before the sales call, and guide employers, workforce boards, and community colleges through journeys built for each of them.

Role
UX Designer
Timeline
16 weeks, 2024
Client
Western Governors University
Industry
Higher education, workforce solutions

Overview

A strong product buried inside a confusing experience

WGU Workforce Solutions offered real value for employers, workforce boards, and community colleges, but the UX failed to communicate it. Different audiences arrived at the same page and left without understanding what partnerships included, how employees benefited, or how to take the next step.

Every partner type landed on the same undifferentiated page, saw the same generic content, and hit the same dead-end "Contact Us" button with nothing to justify the inquiry. The gap between WGU's actual offering and what partners could see online was costing qualified leads at every stage.

When a prospective partner asked what WGU offers, there was no consistent answer. The website showed one thing, marketing materials said another, and the answer could vary wildly depending on who was pitching.

Observed in stakeholder discovery

Three core problems

Three failures were compounding to hold back partner conversion.

  • No audience differentiation

    Employers, workforce boards, and colleges landed on identical pages despite having completely different needs, language, and decision criteria.

  • Unclear product architecture

    Partners couldn't tell what they were buying, what employees would experience, or how WGU differed from competing workforce vendors.

  • No ROI before the sales call

    Decision-makers needed business-case evidence and retention data to build internal buy-in, and none of it existed in the experience.

Research

Three partner types, three distinct journeys

Before designing anything, I needed to understand why partners were landing and leaving without converting. Research ran on three tracks, each surfacing a different layer of the problem.

  • Stakeholder interviews
    • HR and L&D decision-makers at corporate partners
    • Academic leads on community college articulation teams
    • Program officers at government workforce boards
    • Internal WGU sales and partnership teams
  • Competitive analysis
    • Benchmarked Guild Education, Bright Horizons, and Workforce Edge
    • Identified where competitors clearly structured partner value
    • Spotted gaps: no outcome data and no partner-specific routing
    • Captured visual and content differentiation opportunities
  • Analytics and context
    • Market research on retention and replacement costs
    • Vendor differentiation among HR leaders
    • High bounce rates on every Workforce Solutions landing page

What we found

One front door was failing everyone

All three audiences arrived at the same page. With completely different decision criteria and evidence needs, a single page couldn't serve any of them well.

The ROI data existed, just not online

Strong outcome data, case studies, and retention figures lived in sales decks and internal PDFs, but none of it appeared in the digital experience. Partners who needed a business case couldn't find one.

Competitors were much clearer

Guild Education and Bright Horizons offered named tiers, clear feature sets, and outcome data before any sales interaction. WGU compared poorly for partners who had already evaluated the category.

What partners needed to see

85% needed a clear partnership tier structure, 72% needed employee retention ROI data, and 64% needed to understand the downstream employee experience. All three were missing.

Constraints and the decisions they led to

Constraints

  • Three audiences needed fundamentally different experiences without three separate builds
  • Strong ROI data existed in sales decks and PDFs, invisible to any digital visitor
  • Long B2B decision cycles meant supporting return visitors building a business case over weeks
  • Corporate, academic, and workforce board teams each wanted the product presented differently

Decisions

  • A shared component system with partner-specific content modules, chosen by routing logic at entry
  • A content audit run alongside competitive analysis, mapping existing evidence to the moments partners needed it
  • Progressive commitment: the first visit builds credibility, the second delivers the ROI case, the third prompts the inquiry
  • Research synthesis that set a shared problem definition before design, aligning teams in a way meetings couldn't

Solutions

Three features built for the partner decision journey

  • Partner-specific routing

    An audience selector at entry

    All three audiences used to land on the same page. The redesign added an audience selector that routes employers, workforce boards, and colleges into tailored journeys, each with its own messaging, evidence matched to its decision criteria, and calls to action suited to its stage in a long B2B buying cycle.

    Each partner type now sees the evidence most relevant to their decision

  • Structured partnership tiers

    Scannable before contact

    Partners couldn't tell what they were buying or how tiers differed. The redesign introduced named packages, the features included at each level, and outcome data tied to each tier, all visible before any sales conversation. Partners can now self-qualify and arrive at the inquiry already knowing which tier fits.

    Clearer product structure shortens the path to inquiry

  • ROI evidence before the CTA

    The business case, built in

    Decision-makers couldn't build internal buy-in without retention and cost data. Retention ROI, replacement cost savings, and employee outcome metrics now appear above every primary call to action in every partner journey, giving champions the numbers to sell WGU internally before they ever talk to a rep.

    83% task completion for identifying partnership value and next steps in usability testing

  • A generic Contact Us button with no supporting evidence or next-step guidance

After

  • Industry pathway pages so partners can self-identify by workforce need
  • A 4.2× increase in qualified partner inquiries during prototype testing

Reflection

What I'd do differently

The clearest challenge was serving three very different audiences on one platform without fragmenting the experience or tripling the maintenance burden. Routing logic plus a shared component system was the right architecture, but it took discipline. Every content module had to work across partner contexts before we could justify building partner-specific variants on top.

If I started again, I'd run the content audit in week one, not week six. Finding out midway through design that strong ROI data already existed in internal sales decks cost a week of stakeholder back-and-forth. That data was the most valuable design input in the whole project, and finding it earlier would have sharpened the information architecture from the first wireframe.

The other lesson: involve partner representatives in the Define phase, not just Research. Their input on routing logic and tier naming would have sharpened both much earlier. Because they only joined during research, some Define decisions had to be revisited once real partners reviewed the wireframes, adding revision rounds that could have been avoided.
