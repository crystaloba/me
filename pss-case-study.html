01 Overview

A 7-year-old site
holding back real growth

Proper Storage Systems manufactures heavy-duty industrial storage racks capable of holding up to 12,000 lbs. Their products roll-out racks, die storage systems, pneumatic shelving serve the most demanding environments in aerospace, automotive, and OEM manufacturing.

Despite a robust product line and blue-chip clients like Boeing, 3M, and NASA, their website was a conversion bottleneck: non-responsive, visually dated, and lacking the clarity industrial buyers need to act quickly.

"The current site is not responsive. The new site needs to be much lighter and modern looking. Success, for us, is launching a site that works well on all devices and looks far more modern."

— Proper Storage Systems Client

— Problem Statement

How can UX design increase lead generation for B2B industrial websites?

By clarifying the user journey, surfacing strong calls-to-action, and simplifying the RFQ process, UX can guide industrial buyers — who are evaluating on tight timelines — toward requesting a quote, submitting a question, or scheduling a consultation.

02 Research

Understanding the
industrial buyer

Three intersecting research methods informed the redesign. The goal: understand not just what users want to see, but what decisions they're making and how fast they need to make them.

01
Analytics Review
Audited 6 months of site traffic and conversion data to identify where users were dropping off and which pages drove the most leads. The RFQ page was the clear conversion workhorse — but its UX was burying users in complexity.
02
Competitive Analysis
Analyzed Uline, Shelving Inc, Global Industrial, and Rack Engineering Division — examining navigation structures, product presentation hierarchy, and how each site handled customization complexity for B2B buyers.
03
Stakeholder Interviews
One-on-one interviews with internal team members and client stakeholders. Key finding: buyers often arrive with very specific storage problems (die dimensions, load weights) but couldn't find confirmation that PSS could solve them without calling.
— Key Findings
↗ Finding 01
The RFQ form was the most-used but least-understood page
60% of conversions happened on the RFQ page, yet users were abandoning mid-form due to unclear instructions around dimensions and item types. Visual aids were missing.
↗ Finding 02
Mobile users were getting a broken experience
The existing site rendered poorly on any screen smaller than a 1280px desktop. With 38% of B2B buyers now researching on mobile, this was costing leads before they even arrived at a CTA.
↗ Finding 03
Competitors were winning on clarity, not just product
Top-performing competitors in the space used clear product hierarchy, filterable galleries, and prominent trust signals. PSS had the better product but the site didn't reflect it.
↗ Finding 04
Customization was a strength being presented as a burden
PSS's fully custom approach is a differentiator, but the site made the ordering process feel opaque. Buyers need to quickly understand the process, not be overwhelmed by it.

— Constraints
Challenges Navigated
  • Content must be thoroughly audited to prevent incorrect technical specifications from going live
  • Highly customizable products complicate the ordering and quotation interface
  • B2B buyers need rapid access to spec data — information architecture was critical
  • Budget constraints required prioritizing highest-impact UX improvements first
Design Decisions Made
  • Built a modular content system with QA checkpoints before each phase handoff
  • Simplified RFQ with a structured form, visual dimension guides, and "add item" flow
  • Implemented clear product hierarchy and breadcrumb navigation across all pages
  • Focused redesign scope on homepage, product detail pages, RFQ, and gallery first
04 Solutions

Seven features
built for industrial buyers

Transparent Navigation Filterable Photo Gallery Mini Gallery on IPs Consistent Branding Enhanced Search Accessibility Enhancements Responsive Mobile Design
01
Optimized Lead Generation — Detail Page
Streamlined user journeys with persistent "Request a Quote" CTAs, a clearly differentiated value proposition ("Why Work With PSS?"), and a product feature section designed to address the specific concerns of industrial procurement teams. By targeting the unique needs of OEM, aerospace, and automotive buyers, the new site converts research intent into contact intent.
↑ 34% increase in RFQ submissions 6 months post-launch
02
Simplified RFQ — Visual Customization Flow
The original RFQ form had no visual guidance for users unfamiliar with die or cylinder storage specifications. The redesigned page introduced dimensional diagrams for molds, dies, tooling parts, and cylinders — paired with a structured "Item #" entry system that lets users add multiple storage items without friction. A simplified customization process reduces cognitive load and increases form completion.
↓ 22% drop in form abandonment vs. prior RFQ page
03
Filterable Photo Gallery
Industrial clients need to see exactly what they're buying — at scale. The new gallery allows filtering by shelf type (Conveyer Roller, Manual, Pneumatic, Transfer Ball) and storage item category. High-quality images paired with product-level specifications give procurement teams the confidence to move forward without a phone call. For clients managing large operations and tight timelines, this efficiency is material.
↑ 2.4× more gallery page sessions vs. previous static gallery
04
Responsive Design — Mobile-First Architecture
The original site was desktop-only, rendering broken layouts on any device below 1280px wide. The redesign was architected from mobile up, ensuring every page — from the homepage hero to the RFQ form — functions correctly on phones, tablets, and desktops. This addressed the client's primary stated goal and expanded accessible reach to mobile-first industrial buyers.
↑ 51% more mobile sessions engaged bounce rate dropped 18% on mobile

06 Reflection

What I'd do
differently

The clearest win from this project was proving that UX decisions have measurable downstream impact on B2B revenue. A conversion rate improvement from 1.48% to 2.7% on a site receiving over 6,000 visits per half-year is a real number — the kind of outcome that makes the case for design investment to leadership teams in industrial companies that are traditionally skeptical of it.

If I were starting again, I'd push to introduce usability testing in Phase 2, not Phase 4. We made the right calls on the RFQ form and gallery — but those decisions were informed by stakeholder interviews and competitive benchmarking, not direct user observation. Earlier usability testing would have reduced the number of QA-round corrections and created more defensible rationale for design choices during client reviews.

The other lesson: customization complexity is a UX problem disguised as a product problem. Helping industrial buyers understand what they're ordering — before they pick up the phone — is where design can create the most leverage in a B2B manufacturing context.
