Brand guidelines

How Medic looks, sounds, and moves.

Version 1.0November 2026Open Sans + Lora

The brand idea

Medic is a user friendly health management platform. Every dose has a slot, a time, and a tick.

The design starts with a weekly pillbox. A pillbox has a slot for morning, afternoon, evening, and bedtime, so you can see what is done and what is left. Medic borrows that structure for the layout, the shapes, the colors, and the way it talks.

Calm

People open Medic while they manage something serious. It uses quiet color and plenty of space, and it only raises an alarm when a dose is due.

Clear

Each screen does one job. Targets are big, labels sit above fields, and plain words replace medical shorthand.

Kind

A missed dose is information. It gets a small coral note. It never gets a scolding.

Steady

Everything sits where it sat yesterday. When the task is your health, reliable beats clever.

Logo

The Medic logo is the word medic in Lora Bold. A capsule takes the place of the dot on the i. The capsule is half coral and half the color of the word, and it leans 28 degrees.

Specifications

  • Typeface. Lora Bold, lowercase, with letter spacing tightened by 2%.
  • Capsule. 0.23 em wide, 0.46 em tall, corner radius half its width, tilted 28 degrees. Top half Coral 500, bottom half matches the wordmark.
  • Minimum size. Wordmark 72 px wide on screen, 20 mm in print. Capsule mark 24 px.
  • The i. Use the dotless i (U+0131) so the capsule replaces the dot.

Iconography

Icons sit on a 24 px grid with a 2 px rounded stroke and no fills. They take the color of the text beside them. Medicine icons and mood faces are the only filled artwork in the app.

Mood faces

Faces are the one place the app uses full color. Each face has its own fill and a dark outline. Put them on white so the fills stay clear.

Medicine icons

These icons show the form of a medicine. The pill color comes from the tag a person chooses.

Mascot and illustration

Cap is a capsule with a face. Cap shows up where a person might feel unsure, like the help sheets, the assistant, and the success screen. The app has no stock photos and no human figures.

  • Cap speaks in first person with short sentences. Interface labels do not.
  • Cap never appears on error or overdue states. Those stay plain.
  • Illustrations use flat shapes from the palette, with no gradients and no outlines thicker than 3 px.
  • The weekly pill organizer is the main image for use outside the app. Pills rise out of the compartments and drop back in.

Motion

Motion confirms what happened. A pill moving in or out of a compartment is Medic’s one decorative animation.

150 msTaps, hovers, and color changes.
260 msScreen changes. Content rises 10 px and fades in.
320 msBottom sheets slide up from the edge.
600 msThe daily progress ring fills.
EasingUse the curve .2, .8, .2, 1 for movement and a plain ease for fades.
Reduced motionEvery animation switches off when the device asks for it. Nothing depends on motion to make sense.

Voice

Medic talks like a patient pharmacist who has time for you. Short sentences, everyday words, and a clear statement of what happens next.

MEDIC SAYS

Time for your medicine. Lisinopril 5mg, take 1 before a meal.

MEDIC DOES NOT SAY

ALERT. Scheduled dose of Lisinopril is now due.

MEDIC SAYS

All set! I’ll remind you at 6:00 PM.

MEDIC DOES NOT SAY

Medication reminder successfully configured.

  • Say what happened and what comes next. Skip filler like “please note” and “successfully.”
  • Use plain words. Write “side effects,” not “adverse events,” and explain any term you cannot avoid.
  • Never give medical advice in Medic’s own voice. Point people to the label, their pharmacist, or their doctor.
  • Do not scare or shame. A missed dose gets the word “Missed” in small coral type and nothing more.
  • Keep punctuation simple. Use commas and periods, and save exclamation marks for the success screen.

Partner names

When Medic connects to a patient portal, name the portal in plain text, like “Connect MyChart.” Do not use partner logos or claim a partnership until one is approved in writing. Say a connection is available only where it works today.

Accessibility

Many people who manage several medicines are older, and many have low vision or limited dexterity. These rules are part of the brand.

See it

Text meets 4.5 to 1 contrast and graphics meet 3 to 1. Color never carries meaning alone, so every status pairs color with an icon or a word. Larger text mode scales the whole interface.

Tap it

Targets are at least 48 px with 8 px between them. Steppers have big buttons and typed input. Nothing needs a swipe, a long press, or a precise drag.

Hear it

Every icon button has a name. Groups, tabs, switches, and checkboxes use real roles, and updates are announced politely. Focus shows a 3 px teal ring.

Skip it

Animation respects the device’s reduced motion setting. Reminders can be turned off in Settings, and nothing expires while the screen is open.
