01 Overview

50+ years of expertise,
held back by a cluttered site

NLB Corp is one of the world's leading water jetting manufacturing companies, with over 50 years in the industry. Their products serve contractors and operations professionals across aquaculture, surface preparation, hydrodemolition, tank and tote cleaning, and safety gear — globally.

Despite a vast and technically superior product catalog, their website was driving users away. Cluttered navigation, an outdated visual language, and inefficient information architecture made product discovery nearly impossible — and conversion rates reflected it. With 62,228 site visits producing only 683 conversions in a 6-month window, the gap between traffic and leads was significant.

"Navigation is clunky and too much information. It feels like information overload with all the products listed."

— Stakeholder Feedback, NLB Corp UI Audit

— Problem Statement

How can UX design drive increased leads for a complex B2B industrial website?

By improving the user experience through a seamless journey, clear calls-to-action, and optimized forms, UX can guide visitors toward requesting a quote, submitting inquiries, and scheduling consultations — reducing the gap between high organic traffic and low lead volume.

02 Research

Understanding the
industrial buyer journey

Three intersecting research methods informed the redesign strategy. The goal was to understand not just what was broken, but how industrial buyers discover products, evaluate solutions, and ultimately convert — often across multiple sessions and touchpoints.

Research Artifacts

01
Analytics Review
Audited 6 months of traffic and conversion data. Discovered that the RFQ page drove 60% of all conversions — yet the old navigation made finding product-specific RFQ pathways unnecessarily convoluted. Conversion path analysis revealed buyers routinely visited the products page 3+ times before converting, signaling confusion.
02
Competitive Analysis
Analyzed Jetech, Karcher, and Gardner Denver to identify best practices in B2B industrial navigation. Top performers used structured mega-menus with subcategories, persistent quote CTAs, and spec-driven product tables — all of which NLB lacked. Filtering by pressure, flow, and power range was a clear industry standard NLB was missing.
03
User Interviews
One-on-one sessions with the internal team and key stakeholders. Key finding: buyers arrived knowing their application (hydrodemolition, surface prep) but couldn't quickly match it to the right NLB product line. The site's flat product listing — without filterable specs — forced users to either call or leave.
— Key Findings
↗ Finding 01
Navigation was the primary conversion barrier
The existing nav listed all products at the top level with no hierarchy or grouping — creating immediate information overload. Industrial buyers with specific application needs couldn't quickly route themselves to the right product category without scrolling through irrelevant items.
↗ Finding 02
The hero image competed with — not supported — the message
Stakeholder feedback directly called out the hero: a close-up product image used as a decorative banner distracted from the headline rather than reinforcing NLB's scale and authority. The visual hierarchy was undermining credibility at the most critical moment — first impression.
↗ Finding 03
Product specs were buried with no filtering
With 126+ pump unit configurations, buyers needed to filter by pressure range, flow rate, and power output. The old listing page displayed products in an unordered flat list — forcing buyers to read every entry. This was a critical friction point for operations professionals evaluating on tight timelines.
↗ Finding 04
Resource content was unnavigable
NLB had a substantial library of PDFs, spec sheets, and technical literature — but no tagging, filtering, or search. Buyers who landed via organic search (54% of all traffic) looking for specific product documentation often left without converting, even when the content they needed existed on the site.

— Constraints & Decisions
Challenges Navigated
  • Maintaining a consistent experience across devices with a highly complex product catalog of 126+ configurations
  • Adding multiple filters and tags without creating the same visual overload the redesign was trying to eliminate
  • Preserving SEO equity on high-traffic existing pages while restructuring the information architecture
  • Designing a scalable nav system flexible enough to accommodate NLB's expanding target industries
Design Decisions Made
  • Introduced a mega-menu with grouped subcategories — decluttering top-level nav while exposing full depth on hover
  • Built progressive disclosure into the product listing: filters surfaced only when relevant, avoiding upfront overload
  • Retained existing URL structures during IA restructure to protect organic traffic (54% of all visits)
  • Designed an "Industries" nav category to let buyers self-route by application rather than product line
04 Solutions

Five features built
for industrial buyers

Simplified Navigation Filterable Product Listings Tagged Resource Library Persistent Quote CTA Industry-Based Routing
01
Simplified Navigation — Mega-Menu with Subcategories
The original nav presented every product type at the top level, creating a wall of options on arrival. The redesign introduced a structured mega-menu with grouped subcategories (Pumps & Units, Accessories, Parts, Partners) that expand on hover — decluttering the primary nav while preserving full catalog depth. A persistent "My Quote (0)" CTA was placed in the top-right corner on every page, keeping the conversion action always visible.
↓ Reduced top-level nav items by ~60% via subcategory grouping
02
Filterable Product Listings — Spec-Driven Discovery
With 126+ pump unit configurations across pressure (6,000–40,000 psi), flow (4.4–32.5 gpm), and power (125–200 hp) ranges, buyers needed the ability to self-filter without calling a rep. The redesigned product listing page introduced three filter dropdowns (Pressure Range, Flow Range, Power Range) with a live count of matching entries — giving operations professionals the spec-driven discovery tool they expected from an industry leader.
126+ configurations now filterable by pressure, flow, and power range
03
Tagged Resource Library — Searchable PDF Hub
NLB's existing resource content was inaccessible — a flat list of PDFs with no categorization. The redesigned Resource Library introduced a keyword search bar and category filter dropdown, allowing buyers to locate spec sheets, rental brochures, and technical literature by product type. Each resource card displayed a clear category tag (e.g., "Pumps & Units"), file type badge (PDF), and a prominent "View" action — reducing the research friction that was costing NLB organic-traffic conversions.
↑ Resource engagement via search + filter vs. prior unnavigable flat list
04
Redesigned RFQ Form — Structured Quote Flow
The new quote form was rebuilt around the buyer's actual workflow: contact information first, then "Products of Interest" checkboxes (Pumps & Units, Accessories, Parts, Rentals, Training), followed by an additional information message field. Individual product pages gained an "Add to Quote" button directly within spec tables — allowing buyers to build a quote list as they browse, dramatically shortening the path from research to contact intent.
RFQ already drove 60% of conversions redesign targets higher completion rate
05
Industry-Based Routing — Application-First Navigation
A new "Industries" nav category was introduced to let buyers self-identify by application — hydrodemolition, surface preparation, aquaculture, tank and tote cleaning, safety gear — before drilling into products. This addressed the core finding from user interviews: buyers arrived knowing their application need, not their product preference. By routing application-first, the redesign aligned NLB's navigation structure with how industrial buyers actually think.
5 priority industries surfaced in nav vs. 0 on the original site

06 Reflection

What I'd do
differently

The clearest design challenge in this project was scale. NLB Corp's product catalog is genuinely enormous — 126+ pump configurations, dozens of accessory categories, and a technical resource library spanning years of documentation. The risk was that solving the original "information overload" problem would simply relocate the overload rather than resolve it. The mega-menu and progressive filtering approach was the right answer, but it required careful restraint: every new organizational layer had to earn its place.

If I were starting again, I'd push for usability testing specifically with operations professionals and procurement buyers — not just internal stakeholders. The conversion path data showed users visiting the products page 3+ times before converting, which strongly suggests confusion in the product discovery flow. Direct observation of that specific journey would have sharpened the filtering design and potentially surfaced a need for guided product selectors (e.g., "Find My Pump") earlier in the process.

The other learning: the research library is underutilized across B2B industrial sites. NLB had 54% organic traffic — buyers actively searching for water jetting content — and a rich PDF library that wasn't surfaced effectively. In a future phase, turning that resource library into a gated lead magnet could meaningfully increase the conversion rate by capturing intent that currently passes through without leaving contact information.
